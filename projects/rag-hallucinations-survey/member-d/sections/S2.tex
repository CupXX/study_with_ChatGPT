\subsection{Review Scope and Literature Identification}
This survey addresses the causes and manifestations of hallucination across the four working stages of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), together with available detection and mitigation evidence. The stage framework is an organising device for research synthesis rather than a claim that every observable error has one uniquely identifiable origin. To support transparent reporting, the review plan draws on relevant elements of PRISMA 2020 \cite{page2021prisma}, while recognising that use of a reporting guideline does not by itself establish formal systematic-review compliance.

The preliminary literature identification documented for the \emph{Generation and Knowledge Utilisation} chapter used targeted web searches on 20 October 2026, including searches restricted to the ACL Anthology and searches for original ICLR conference papers. Queries included the terms \emph{RAG}, \emph{knowledge conflict}, \emph{citation}, \emph{generation}, \emph{multi-hop reasoning}, \emph{sufficient context}, and \emph{abstention}; the actual example query strings and source links are retained in the Member D search log. This process identified seven papers whose official proceedings records and original full texts were consulted for the present chapter. The search was targeted, not demonstrated to be exhaustive. \subsection{Eligibility, Screening and Study Set}
In the preliminary Stage~4 work, sources were considered for their ability to support identifiable claims about how RAG systems use or fail to use retrieved material during generation. Conference or publisher metadata was checked, and relevant original-paper sections were examined to establish experimental setups, findings, and limitations. The resulting seven-paper \emph{chapter evidence set} contains mechanism studies, generation-side interventions, and supporting evaluation resources. An end-to-end RAG benchmark was not treated as direct proof of a generation-specific causal origin, and general language-model evidence was not silently relabelled as RAG-specific evidence.
